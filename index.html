Score Proposals on Coverage, Not Price Alone

Give each written quote a simple coverage check before discussing cost. A comparable proposal should itemise:

  • excavation depth and soil removal, including spoil handling off site
  • foundations or post footings, stated with the wall system they support
  • wall materials with profile, spacing and finish specified
  • drainage, backfill and the intended water outlet
  • corners, curves and levels where the site geometry demands them
  • engineering, certification and approval items, marked included, excluded or provisional
  • GST, reinstatement and exclusions, confirmed in writing

A missing line is not automatic grounds to discard a builder. It is grounds to ask a direct question, because a scope that stays silent on drainage behind a loaded wall is a scope you cannot yet trust.

Match the Wall System to the Constraint

The choice between concrete sleeper, block and timber sleeper construction should follow the site, not habit. Ask each builder to state which constraint drove their recommendation.

  • Height and load: higher or loaded walls suit systems with engineered footing detail and certifier input before construction begins.
  • Exposure: walls catching heavy runoff need drainage stated as a designed component, not an afterthought.
  • Access: narrow gates, steps or overhead limits shape equipment choice and spoil removal cost.
  • Lifespan expectation: treated timber can suit modest heights with realistic maintenance; concrete and block systems suit longer horizons.
  • Repair versus replacement: where an existing wall leans, cracks or shows drainage failure, ask what the inspection actually found before accepting either answer.

Removal scopes deserve the same rigour: demolition, temporary support, access, spoil and the drainage-backed replacement system should each appear in writing.

Locality Gives Context, Not Verdicts

A slope in Rangeville, a boundary wall in Middle Ridge and a yard in Highfields share the same planning logic but rarely the same site conditions. Nearby structures, loaded ground and access constraints can each change the scrutiny a job needs. Confirm requirements for the actual property with a licensed certifier before work starts, since approval risk attaches to the address and design, not the suburb name alone. Also note that submitting an enquiry confirms nothing about provider availability, timing, acceptance or price; suitability and cost are assessed once the real project details are reviewed.
